**AIXI** [/ˈaɪksi/](/wiki/Help:IPA/English "Help:IPA/English") is a theoretical [mathematical formalism](/wiki/Mathematical_logic#Formal_logical_systems "Mathematical logic") for [artificial general intelligence](/wiki/Artificial_general_intelligence "Artificial general intelligence").
It combines [Solomonoff induction](/wiki/Solomonoff_induction "Solomonoff induction") with [sequential decision theory](/wiki/Decision_theory "Decision theory").
AIXI was first proposed by [Marcus Hutter](/wiki/Marcus_Hutter "Marcus Hutter") in 2000[[1]](#cite_note-1) and several results regarding AIXI are proved in Hutter's 2005 book *Universal Artificial Intelligence*.[[2]](#cite_note-uaibook-2)

AIXI is a [reinforcement learning](/wiki/Reinforcement_learning "Reinforcement learning") (RL) agent. It maximizes the expected total rewards received from the environment. Intuitively, it simultaneously considers every computable hypothesis (or environment). In each time step, it looks at every possible program and evaluates how many rewards that program generates depending on the next action taken. The promised rewards are then weighted by the [subjective belief](/wiki/Subjective_logic "Subjective logic") that this program constitutes the true environment. This belief is computed from the length of the program: longer programs are considered less likely, in line with [Occam's razor](/wiki/Occam%27s_razor "Occam's razor"). AIXI then selects the action that has the highest expected total reward in the weighted sum of all these programs.

## Etymology

According to Hutter, the word "AIXI" can have several interpretations. AIXI can stand for AI based on Solomonoff's distribution, denoted by

{\displaystyle \xi }
![{\displaystyle \xi }](https://wikimedia.org/api/rest_v1/media/math/render/svg/e0b461aaf61091abd5d2c808931c48b8ff9647db) (which is the Greek letter xi), or e.g. it can stand for AI "crossed" (X) with induction (I). There are other interpretations.[[3]](#cite_note-:0-3)

### Parameters

The parameters to AIXI are the universal Turing machine *U* and the agent's lifetime *m*, which need to be chosen. The latter parameter can be removed by the use of [discounting](/wiki/Discounting "Discounting").

## Optimality

AIXI's performance is measured by the expected total number of rewards it receives.
AIXI has been proven to be optimal in the following ways.[[2]](#cite_note-uaibook-2)

- [Pareto optimality](/wiki/Pareto_optimality "Pareto optimality"): there is no other agent that performs at least as well as AIXI in all environments while performing strictly better in at least one environment.[*[citation needed](/wiki/Wikipedia:Citation_needed "Wikipedia:Citation needed")*]
- Balanced Pareto optimality: like Pareto optimality, but considering a weighted sum of environments.
- Self-optimizing: a policy *p* is called self-optimizing for an environment

  μ
  {\displaystyle \mu }
  ![{\displaystyle \mu }](https://wikimedia.org/api/rest_v1/media/math/render/svg/9fd47b2a39f7a7856952afec1f1db72c67af6161) if the performance of *p* approaches the theoretical maximum for

  μ
  {\displaystyle \mu }
  ![{\displaystyle \mu }](https://wikimedia.org/api/rest_v1/media/math/render/svg/9fd47b2a39f7a7856952afec1f1db72c67af6161) when the length of the agent's lifetime (not time) goes to infinity. For environment classes where self-optimizing policies exist, AIXI is self-optimizing.

It was later shown by Hutter and [Jan Leike](/wiki/Jan_Leike "Jan Leike") that balanced Pareto optimality is subjective and that any policy can be considered Pareto optimal, which they describe as undermining all previous optimality claims for AIXI.[[5]](#cite_note-5)

However, AIXI does have limitations. It is restricted to maximizing rewards based on percepts as opposed to external states. It also assumes it interacts with the environment solely through action and percept channels, preventing it from considering the possibility of being damaged or modified. Colloquially, this means that it doesn't consider itself to be contained by the environment it interacts with. It also assumes the environment is computable.[[6]](#cite_note-6)

## Computational aspects

Like [Solomonoff induction](/wiki/Solomonoff_induction "Solomonoff induction"), AIXI is [incomputable](/wiki/Undecidable_problem "Undecidable problem"). However, there are computable approximations of it. One such approximation is AIXI*tl*, which performs at least as well as the provably best time *t* and space *l* limited agent.[[2]](#cite_note-uaibook-2) Another approximation to AIXI with a restricted environment class is MC-AIXI (FAC-CTW) (which stands for [Monte Carlo](/wiki/Monte_Carlo_method "Monte Carlo method") AIXI FAC-[Context-Tree Weighting](/wiki/Context_tree_weighting "Context tree weighting")), which has had some success playing simple games such as [partially observable](/wiki/Partially_observable_system "Partially observable system") [Pac-Man](/wiki/Pac-Man "Pac-Man").[[4]](#cite_note-veness2009-4)[[7]](#cite_note-7)
