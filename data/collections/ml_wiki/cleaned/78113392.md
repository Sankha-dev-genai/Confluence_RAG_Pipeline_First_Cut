1943 paper proposing artificial neural networks

"**A Logical Calculus of the Ideas Immanent in Nervous Activity**" is a 1943 paper written by [Warren Sturgis McCulloch](/wiki/Warren_Sturgis_McCulloch "Warren Sturgis McCulloch") and [Walter Pitts](/wiki/Walter_Pitts "Walter Pitts"), published in the journal *[The Bulletin of Mathematical Biophysics](/wiki/The_Bulletin_of_Mathematical_Biophysics "The Bulletin of Mathematical Biophysics")*.[[1]](#cite_note-1) The paper proposed a mathematical model of the nervous system as a network of simple logical elements, later known as [artificial neurons](/wiki/Artificial_neuron "Artificial neuron"), or [McCulloch–Pitts neurons](/wiki/McCulloch%E2%80%93Pitts_neuron "McCulloch–Pitts neuron"). These neurons receive inputs, perform a weighted sum, and fire an output signal based on a threshold function. By connecting these units in various configurations, McCulloch and Pitts demonstrated that their model could perform all logical functions.

It is a seminal work in [cognitive science](/wiki/Cognitive_science "Cognitive science"), [computational neuroscience](/wiki/Computational_neuroscience "Computational neuroscience"), [computer science](/wiki/Computer_science "Computer science"), and [artificial intelligence](/wiki/Artificial_intelligence "Artificial intelligence"). It was a foundational result in [automata theory](/wiki/Automata_theory "Automata theory"). [John von Neumann](/wiki/John_von_Neumann "John von Neumann") cited it as a significant result.[[2]](#cite_note-:0-2)

## Context

### Previous work

The paper built upon several previous strands of work.[[5]](#cite_note-5)[[6]](#cite_note-6)

In the symbolic logic side, it built on the previous work by Carnap, Whitehead, and Russell. This was contributed by Walter Pitts, who had a strong proficiency with symbolic logic. Pitts provided mathematical and logical rigor to McCulloch’s vague ideas on psychons (atoms of psychological events) and circular causality.[[7]](#cite_note-7)

In the neuroscience side, it built on previous work by the mathematical biology research group centered around [Nicolas Rashevsky](/wiki/Nicolas_Rashevsky "Nicolas Rashevsky"), of which McCulloch was a member. The paper was published in the *Bulletin of Mathematical Biophysics*, which was founded by Rashevsky in 1939. During the late 1930s, Rashevsky's research group was producing papers that had difficulty publishing in other journals at the time, so Rashevsky decided to found a new journal exclusively devoted to mathematical biophysics.[[8]](#cite_note-8)

Also in the Rashevsky's group was [Alston Scott Householder](/wiki/Alston_Scott_Householder "Alston Scott Householder"), who in 1941 published an abstract model of the steady-state activity of biological neural networks. The model, in modern language, is an artificial neural network with [ReLU](/wiki/Rectifier_(neural_networks) "Rectifier (neural networks)") activation function.[[9]](#cite_note-9) In a series of papers, Householder calculated the stable states of very simple networks: a chain, a circle, and a [bouquet](/wiki/Rose_(topology) "Rose (topology)"). Walter Pitts' first two papers formulated a mathematical theory of learning and conditioning. The next three were mathematical developments of Householder’s model.[[10]](#cite_note-:1-10)

In 1938, at age 15, Pitts ran away from home in Detroit and arrived in the [University of Chicago](/wiki/University_of_Chicago "University of Chicago"). Later, he walked into Rudolf Carnap's office with Carnap's book filled with corrections and suggested improvements. He started studying under Carnap and attending classes during 1938–1943. He wrote several early papers on neuronal network modelling and regularly attended Rashevsky's seminars in theoretical biology. The seminar attendants included Gerhard von Bonin and Householder. In 1940, von Bonin introduced Lettvin to McCulloch. In 1942, both Lettvin and Pitts had moved in with McCulloch's home.[[11]](#cite_note-11)

McCulloch had been interested in circular causality from studies with [causalgia](/wiki/Complex_regional_pain_syndrome "Complex regional pain syndrome") after amputation, [epileptic](/wiki/Epilepsy "Epilepsy") activity of surgically isolated brain, and [Lorente de Nò](/wiki/Rafael_Lorente_de_N%C3%B3 "Rafael Lorente de Nó")'s research showing [recurrent neural networks](/wiki/Recurrent_neural_network "Recurrent neural network") are needed to explain [vestibular nystagmus](/wiki/Vestibulo-ocular_reflex "Vestibulo-ocular reflex"). He had difficulty with treating circular causality until Pitts demonstrated how it can be treated by the appropriate mathematical tools of [modular arithmetics](/wiki/Modular_arithmetic "Modular arithmetic") and symbolic logic.[[4]](#cite_note-number3-4)[[10]](#cite_note-:1-10)

Both authors' affiliation in the article was given as "University of Illinois, College of Medicine, Department of Psychiatry at the Illinois Neuropsychiatric Institute, University of Chicago, Chicago, U.S.A."

### Subsequent work

It was a foundational result in [automata theory](/wiki/Automata_theory "Automata theory"). [John von Neumann](/wiki/John_von_Neumann "John von Neumann") cited it as a significant result.[[2]](#cite_note-:0-2) This work led to work on neural networks and their link to [finite automata](/wiki/Finite_automata "Finite automata").[[12]](#cite_note-12) Kleene introduced the term *"regular"* for "regular language" in a 1951 technical report, where Kleene proved that [regular languages](/wiki/Regular_language "Regular language") are all that could be generated by neural networks, among other results. The term "regular" was meant to be suggestive of "regularly occurring events" that the neural net automaton must process and respond to.[[13]](#cite_note-13)

[Marvin Minsky](/wiki/Marvin_Minsky "Marvin Minsky") was influenced by McCulloch, built an early example of neural network [SNARC](/wiki/Stochastic_Neural_Analog_Reinforcement_Calculator "Stochastic Neural Analog Reinforcement Calculator") (1951), and did a PhD thesis on neural networks (1954).[[14]](#cite_note-14)

McCulloch was the chair to the ten [Macy conferences](/wiki/Macy_conferences "Macy conferences") (1946–1953) on "Circular Causal and Feedback Mechanisms in Biological and Social Systems". This was a key event in the beginning of [cybernetics](/wiki/Cybernetics "Cybernetics"), and what later became known as [cognitive science](/wiki/Cognitive_science "Cognitive science"). Pitts also attended the conferences.[[15]](#cite_note-15)

In the 1943 paper, they described how memories can be formed by a neural network with loops in it, or alterable synapses, which are operating over time, and implements [logical universals](/wiki/Universal_quantification "Universal quantification") -- "there exists" and "for all". This was generalized for spatial objects, such as geometric figures, in their 1947 paper *How we know universals*.[[16]](#cite_note-16) Norbert Wiener found this a significant evidence for a general method for how animals recognizing objects, by scanning a scene from multiple transformations and finding a canonical representation. He hypothesized that this "scanning" activity is clocked by the [alpha wave](/wiki/Alpha_wave "Alpha wave"), which he mistakenly thought was tightly regulated at 10 Hz (instead of the 8 -- 13 Hz as modern research shows).[[17]](#cite_note-17)

McCulloch worked with [Manuel Blum](/wiki/Manuel_Blum "Manuel Blum") in studying how a neural network can be "logically stable", that is, can implement a boolean function even if the activation thresholds of individual neurons are varied.[[18]](#cite_note-18): 64 They were inspired by the problem of how the brain can perform the same functions, such as breathing, under influence of [caffeine](/wiki/Caffeine "Caffeine") or [alcohol](/wiki/Ethanol "Ethanol"), which shifts the activation threshold over the entire brain.[[4]](#cite_note-number3-4)
