**Dataupia** was a supplier of [data warehouse appliances](/wiki/Data_warehouse_appliance "Data warehouse appliance"). Dataupia focuses on [data warehousing](/wiki/Data_warehouse "Data warehouse") for applications running on [Oracle](/wiki/Oracle_Corporation "Oracle Corporation"), [Microsoft SQL Server](/wiki/Microsoft_SQL_Server "Microsoft SQL Server") databases. Dataupia's Satori Server included server computers, storage, and software.

## History

Dataupia was founded in 2005 by Foster D. Hinshaw for analytics in "[big data](/wiki/Big_data "Big data")" applications such as for telecom and auto-reporting for the [Internet of things](/wiki/Internet_of_things "Internet of things").

A classic definition of the [data warehouse](/wiki/Data_warehouse "Data warehouse") focuses on data storage.[[1]](#cite_note-InmonDefinition-1) However, the means to retrieve and analyze data, to [extract, transform and load](/wiki/Extract,_transform_and_load "Extract, transform and load") data, and to manage the dictionary data are also considered essential components of a [data warehouse appliance](/wiki/Data_warehouse_appliance "Data warehouse appliance") system. The Dataupia Satori Server provided all aspects of the [data warehouse appliance](/wiki/Data_warehouse_appliance "Data warehouse appliance") in a single product.

The company has headquarters in [Cambridge, Massachusetts](/wiki/Cambridge,_Massachusetts "Cambridge, Massachusetts"), with additional offices in Binghamton, United Kingdom. The company was privately held.[[2]](#cite_note-DataupiaFactSheet-2)

Dataupia's name is a [portmanteau](/wiki/Portmanteau "Portmanteau") of the words "data", in reference to the data warehousing industry, and "[utopia](/wiki/Utopia "Utopia")". The combination of words is used by the company to imply ideal access to data.

The Dataupia flagship product, Satori Server, is a network-attached [data warehouse appliance](/wiki/Data_warehouse_appliance "Data warehouse appliance") announced in 2007.[[3]](#cite_note-ProductReview1-3) As an appliance, it includes an embedded copy of Linux, a database engine, an aggregation engine, built-in storage, and parallel processors. In contrast to other custom-assembly data warehouse appliances, the Satori Server worked with existing database management systems, rather than requiring them to be replaced. In theory, this would appeal to customers with a huge investment in databases and data warehouse and [business intelligence](/wiki/Business_intelligence "Business intelligence") applications.[[4]](#cite_note-DataupiaLaunch-4)[[5]](#cite_note-SatoriServer-5)
Competitors included [Teradata](/wiki/Teradata "Teradata") and [IBM](/wiki/IBM "IBM") (which acquired [Netezza](/wiki/Netezza "Netezza") in 2010).[[6]](#cite_note-6)

In January 2009 Hinshaw temporarily left due to health problems, and Tony Sirianni became chief executive through November.[[7]](#cite_note-7)
During the [Great Recession](/wiki/Great_Recession "Great Recession"), the company reduced its staff and by August 2009 was seeking a buyer for its assets.[[8]](#cite_note-8)
By 2017, the company had not issued a press release since the 4.0 version of the product was announced in February 2011.[[9]](#cite_note-9)
